Title: Agroforestry Parklands
Category: Crop Resilience / Heat and Soil Restoration
Problem Solved: Improving farm resilience by combining trees with crops or livestock.
Historical Origin: Tree-crop systems are widespread traditional practices. Examples include Sahelian parklands, home gardens, alley cropping, and mixed orchard-field systems.
How It Worked: Trees provide shade, organic matter, wind protection, fodder, fuel, fruit, nitrogen fixation in some species, and deeper nutrient cycling.
Evidence It Worked: Modern agroecology supports many agroforestry benefits, but outcomes depend on tree species, density, water competition, land tenure, and farmer incentives.
Why It Declined: Monoculture incentives, mechanization, short-term yield targets, insecure land rights, and removal of trees from fields reduced adoption.
Modern Revival Potential: Climate-smart agriculture programs, remote sensing, carbon finance, nursery networks, and locally selected species can revive agroforestry.
Risks: Wrong species can compete with crops or become invasive. Benefits may take years.
Verdict: Worth Reviving with local species and farmer-led design.
Source Notes: Agroecology, dryland restoration, and climate-smart agriculture literature.
