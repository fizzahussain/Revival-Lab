Title: Earth Air Tunnels
Category: Passive Cooling / Ground-Coupled Ventilation
Problem Solved: Reducing cooling demand by pre-cooling incoming air using the relatively stable temperature of the ground.
Historical Origin: Ground-coupled cooling principles appear in earth-sheltered structures, underground houses, qanat-linked cooling, and vernacular systems in hot dry regions.
How It Worked: Outside air passes through buried pipes or underground channels before entering a building. Heat transfers from the air into cooler surrounding soil, reducing indoor temperature.
Evidence It Worked: Modern building science supports ground-coupled heat exchange when pipe length, burial depth, airflow rate, drainage, and hygiene are designed correctly.
Why It Declined: Mechanical cooling became easier to install, while underground systems require careful construction, cleaning, and moisture control.
Modern Revival Potential: Modern pipes, sensors, fans, antimicrobial materials, condensate drainage, and simulation tools can make the idea safer and more reliable.
Risks: Poorly maintained tunnels can develop mold, pests, or stagnant air. Humid climates require careful drainage and filtration.
Verdict: Worth Reviving with strict design controls.
Source Notes: Passive cooling and earth-air heat exchanger research literature.
