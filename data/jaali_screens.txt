Title: Jaali Screens and Perforated Shading
Category: Passive Cooling / Solar Control
Problem Solved: Reducing heat gain, glare, and privacy problems in hot buildings while allowing filtered daylight and airflow.
Historical Origin: Perforated stone, brick, terracotta, and wooden screens appear in South Asian, Islamic, and Middle Eastern architecture. In South Asia, jaali screens became famous in Mughal and Indo-Islamic buildings but similar lattice logic exists in many hot-climate traditions.
How It Worked: Small openings break harsh sunlight into diffuse light, reduce direct solar radiation, and preserve privacy. When combined with courtyards and shaded openings, they support airflow while reducing glare and heat.
Evidence It Worked: The physical principle is strong: shading lowers solar heat gain, and porous facades can support ventilation. Performance depends on opening ratio, orientation, wind, wall material, and maintenance.
Why It Declined: Modern glass facades, sealed air-conditioned buildings, cheaper industrial materials, and changing architectural tastes reduced their use.
Modern Revival Potential: Jaali-inspired facades can be redesigned using parametric geometry, 3D printed panels, local clay or concrete, and daylight simulation. They are especially useful for schools, libraries, and public buildings.
Risks: Poor designs can block too much daylight or airflow. Dust accumulation and cleaning must be planned.
Verdict: Worth Reviving.
Source Notes: General architectural passive design literature; compare with passive cooling reviews and vernacular architecture case studies.
