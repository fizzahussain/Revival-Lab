Title: Mangrove Buffers
Category: Coastal Flooding / Storm Protection
Problem Solved: Reducing coastal erosion, storm surge impact, and habitat loss.
Historical Origin: Coastal communities have long relied on mangrove ecosystems for fisheries, fuelwood, shoreline stability, and protection.
How It Worked: Mangrove roots and trunks slow waves, trap sediment, support biodiversity, and stabilize shorelines.
Evidence It Worked: Nature-based coastal protection literature supports mangroves as part of flood risk reduction, but effectiveness depends on width, species, health, hydrology, and storm intensity.
Why It Declined: Coastal development, pollution, aquaculture, cutting, and altered hydrology destroyed many mangrove belts.
Modern Revival Potential: Restoration can combine community stewardship, remote sensing, legal protection, and hybrid seawall-mangrove designs.
Risks: Planting in the wrong place fails. Mangroves need suitable salinity, tidal flow, and sediment conditions.
Verdict: Worth Reviving where ecological conditions fit.
Source Notes: Nature-based coastal resilience and ecosystem restoration literature.
