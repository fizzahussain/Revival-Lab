Title: Organic Mulching
Category: Crop Resilience / Soil Moisture
Problem Solved: Conserving soil moisture, reducing evaporation, suppressing weeds, and improving soil organic matter.
Historical Origin: Farmers in many regions traditionally covered soil with straw, leaves, crop residues, manure, or other organic matter.
How It Worked: Mulch shields soil from direct sun and wind, slows evaporation, buffers temperature, and gradually decomposes into organic matter.
Evidence It Worked: Agronomy supports mulching benefits, although results depend on crop, climate, mulch material, pest pressure, and labor availability.
Why It Declined: Crop residues are often burned, grazed, removed for fuel, or displaced by input-intensive farming.
Modern Revival Potential: Combine mulching with conservation agriculture, composting, drip irrigation, and local residue management.
Risks: Can harbor pests, tie up nitrogen temporarily, or conflict with livestock feed needs.
Verdict: Worth Reviving with local material planning.
Source Notes: Soil conservation and sustainable agriculture literature.
