Title: Rain Gardens
Category: Urban Flooding / Water Quality
Problem Solved: Capturing roof and street runoff close to where rain falls.
Historical Origin: Rain gardens are a modern named practice, but they revive older landscape logic: let water soak into soil instead of rushing away through hard channels.
How It Worked: Depressed planted areas temporarily hold stormwater, filter it through soil and roots, and reduce peak runoff.
Evidence It Worked: Urban resilience and green infrastructure literature supports distributed bioretention when sizing, soil mix, overflow routes, and plant selection match local rainfall.
Why It Declined or Was Overlooked: Conventional urban design often prioritized paved surfaces and underground drains over visible planted water systems.
Modern Revival Potential: Useful for schools, homes, campuses, sidewalks, and public parks. Can be combined with rain barrels and permeable paving.
Risks: Poor maintenance, mosquito concerns, compacted soil, and lack of overflow planning can undermine performance.
Verdict: Worth Reviving.
Source Notes: Nature-based solutions and urban stormwater management literature.
