Title: Shaded Courtyard Microclimates
Category: Passive Cooling / Urban Design
Problem Solved: Creating cooler indoor-outdoor transition spaces in dense hot-climate buildings.
Historical Origin: Courtyard houses appear across the Middle East, North Africa, South Asia, Mediterranean regions, and other climates.
How It Worked: Courtyards regulate light, shade, airflow, and thermal mass. Vegetation and water features can further cool the microclimate.
Evidence It Worked: Passive cooling literature supports courtyards when proportions, orientation, shading, materials, and ventilation match local climate.
Why It Declined: Apartment towers, land pressure, sealed HVAC buildings, and modern plot economics reduced courtyard use.
Modern Revival Potential: Apartment blocks, schools, campuses, and public buildings can reinterpret courtyards as shaded ventilation and social spaces.
Risks: Bad proportions can trap heat. Humid climates need airflow and moisture control.
Verdict: Worth Reviving with climate-specific design.
Source Notes: Passive cooling reviews and vernacular architecture literature.
