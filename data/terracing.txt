Title: Agricultural Terracing
Category: Crop Resilience / Soil and Water Conservation
Problem Solved: Reducing erosion, slowing runoff, and making sloped land productive.
Historical Origin: Terracing appears independently in many mountainous and hilly societies, including Andean, East Asian, Mediterranean, African, and Himalayan landscapes.
How It Worked: Slopes are reshaped into stepped fields. The steps slow water, reduce soil loss, create manageable growing surfaces, and can improve moisture retention.
Evidence It Worked: Long-lived terrace landscapes show that the principle can be effective, but success depends on maintenance, drainage, slope stability, and local labor systems.
Why It Declined: Rural depopulation, labor intensity, mechanized farming, land abandonment, and maintenance failures caused many terraces to degrade.
Modern Revival Potential: GIS mapping, community labor planning, check dams, agroforestry, and climate-resilient crops can support terrace revival.
Risks: Poorly built terraces can fail, increase landslide risk, or concentrate runoff.
Verdict: Worth Reviving where slope, community capacity, and maintenance plans are suitable.
Source Notes: Soil conservation, watershed management, and traditional agricultural landscape studies.
