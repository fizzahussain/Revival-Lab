Title: Traditional Drainage Channels
Category: Urban Flooding / Water-Sensitive Settlement Design
Problem Solved: Moving stormwater safely while slowing and spreading it before it becomes destructive.
Historical Origin: Older settlements often used open channels, street slopes, tanks, ponds, and low-lying overflow areas to manage monsoon or seasonal water.
How It Worked: Water was guided by gravity through visible channels and storage points instead of hidden only underground.
Evidence It Worked: The logic remains relevant: visible, maintainable drainage can reduce blockage risk when combined with storage and infiltration.
Why It Declined: Paved roads, encroachment, underground drains, trash accumulation, and loss of ponds or wetlands broke traditional drainage networks.
Modern Revival Potential: Restore blue-green corridors, daylight drains, add trash screens, map blocked channels, and combine with rain gardens and bioswales.
Risks: Open drains can be unsafe or unsanitary if wastewater mixes with stormwater.
Verdict: Worth Reviving as clean stormwater corridors, not as polluted open drains.
Source Notes: Urban water-sensitive design and nature-based solutions literature.
